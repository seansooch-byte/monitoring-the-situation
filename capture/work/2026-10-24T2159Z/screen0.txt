So when the issue is not election fraud, it becomes election interference by the Executive Branch. Can the Executive Branch use allegations of voter fraud to interfere with the state certification process or obstruct the formation of a newly elected Congress. I submit to you, that the answer is no on both counts.\nThe United States Constitution under the  Tenth Amendment, which states that powers not given to the federal government, and not forbidden to the states, belong to the states or to the people. Under this amendment and the Election Clause (Article I, Section 4, Clause 1) states have sole control of their elections and the Executive Branch including the DOJ cannot subvert the election process or the certification process. Thesese responsibilities cannot simply be rearranged whenever an election becomes politically inconvenient.\nThe Justice Department is not a national election board. The attorney general does not get to decide who wins congressional elections. State Election Certification Is Not Todd Blanche’s Decision\nAs mentioned, Article I, Section 4 of the Constitution gives states a central role in establishing and administering the procedures for congressional elections. Congress has the right to make laws for states to follow, but in the absence of such laws states operate within the parameters of existing laws. It is under these conditions that Todd Blanche has absolutely no power here.\nState and local officials administer elections, count votes, and certify results according to the laws that govern them. The federal government's job is to protect voting rights and prosecute attempts to violate election laws. That power does not invite them to usurp the power over state elections or state certifications.\nThe DOJ Justice Manual states this in Section 9-85.300 where states are responsible for administering elections, determining the validity of votes, and tabulating results. It even warns that the department has a limited role and should avoid interfering with the election process barring any massive increase in voter fraud backed up by a mountain of evidence. Even then, it can only submit a complaint to the court and asked the court to stop the certification for an investigation. Even that only applies to precincts where they have compelling evidence.\nSource: U.S. Department of Justice, Justice Manual, Section 9-85.30\nIn other words, Blanch cannot simply order a state(s) to stop certifying an election because the DOJ suspects something is wrong. Suspecting something is wrong doesn't make something wrong. If there is reason to suspect something is wrong and there is enough evidence to justify an investigation, the DOJ can pursue remedies already prescribed into law. This would mean taking legal action within the precinct where a judge might prescribe relief with credible evidence.  However, any intervention must rely on legal authority, and he does not have such authority to act alone. The power to investigate a crime is not the same as having the power to control an election's outcome.\n2. A Challenge to One Seat Is Not a License to Block an Entire Congress\nElection disputes must be handled according to the law. If credible evidence supports a challenge to a disputed seat(s), such a dispute can be investigated through the appropriate state, judicial, and Congressional procedures.\nThe Court system does play a legitimate role in election integrity. It can issue an order that will affect a race, but an allegation is not proof, and any judge is going to require credible evidence to invalidate votes or suspend an election.\nThe Constitution also establishes a specific safeguard for congressional membership. Article I, Section 5 states that the both Houses maintain the constitutional authority to resolve contests of its own members. They are the ones who ultimately vote and decide who is entitled to a contested seat.\nSource: U.S. House of Representatives, Contested Elections\nThere is a difference between a legitimate dispute over a particular election and an attempt to obstruct the formation of the legislative branch as a whole.  A dispute in one district cannot invalidate other districts, nor can it prevent the Speaker of the House from exercising their duty on January 3rd post election. Neither Todd Blanche nor the president has independent constitutional authority to decide that an entire incoming Congress cannot serve.\n3. Congressional Terms Expire. They Do Not Depend on Political Permission.\nOne of the most important election safeguards is found in the Twentieth Amendment to the Constitution.\nSection 1 provides that the terms of senators and representatives end at noon on January 3, in the years their terms expire, and that the terms of their successors begin at that same time. This is written and it is law. It is not a suggestion and something other branches can interfere with. Those who lose can't stay longer while their seat is contested. Their term is expired. Congress may decide not to seat the disputed incoming member until the election plays out. The same applies to the Senate, but only those whose  6 year term has actually expired. House members all serve 2 year terms, so all of them who lost are out on January 3rd post election.\nSource: Twentieth Amendment, Sections 1–2\nSimply put, Congress cannot extend their time indefinitely. The courts, nor the executive branch can extend the House terms. They are fixed, and they are final. All challenges must be resolved through lawful procedures. They do not grant outgoing officials a permanent hold on power.\n4. The New House Can Organize Without the Dean’s Permission\nThe new Speaker of the House who was elected Speaker prior to the swearing in,  is sworn in by the Dean of Congress in a ceremonial procedure. If the Republican dean chooses not to, it does not stop the new Speaker from assuming his role. The New Speaker will be the one to swear in the new Congress. A ceremonial tradition cannot be turned into a weapon from preventing the House from organizing\nSource: United States Code, 2 U.S.C. § 25\n5. The Constitution Does Not Give the Executive Branch Control Over Congress\nOur government has 3 branches of government and no branch possesses unlimited authority over another. State and local officials run elections under existing law. Congress has the authority to judge the elections and the qualifications of its own members. The executive branch enforces election, and the judicial branch prosecutes violations of election laws. Occasionally these responsibilities intersect. That does not make them interchangeable.\nCourts can resolve disputes, but they cannot replace members as that is Congress's responsibility. Todd Blanche and Donald Trump do not inherit the Houses's authority over such matters. \"national emergency,\" \"fraud,\" or even \"doomsday scenario' do not create such authority.\nIt is for these reasons that Todd Blanche's comments should be concerning. America has held elections every four years since its inception. We have voted through many wars, a Great Depression, and deep recessions. There truly is nothing that could stop an election. A nuclear attack in New York doesn't stop elections in other states. New York just won't be seated until members are validly chosen by the people. So why does Blanche state these words? Does he know something we do not? Is this administration planning something we are unaware of? Or, is Blanche just sowing fear into the minds of voters?\n6. The Peaceful Transfer of Legislative Power Must Be Protected\nFor a democracy to thrive, it cannot be just about casting ballots. It must require public officials to respect the process and the procedures including: how the votes are counted, certified, challenged, and turned into representation. There will be disputes every now and then, but those disputes must be handled according to the law. One branch of government does not get to seize the powers of another branch to hold onto a slim majority.\nIf an administration can simply seize powers not theirs based on unproven allegations, public confidence in the system is destroyed. If losers can become winners simply by contesting their defeats, elections lose all credibility and who will want to participate in a rigged system? The beauty of the Constitution is that it is  designed to prevent any type of concentration of power among branches.\nConclusion: The Constitution Sets the Limits\nTodd Blanche has the authority and responsibility to enforce federal law within the limits established by the Constitution and statutes, but he lacks the authority  to determine election outcomes. Courts have the sole authority to resolve legal disputes, but ultimately Congress decides who gets to sit.\nCongressional terms expire on a constitutional schedule and not on a whim. The House chooses its Speaker. The House and Senate exercise their own constitutional responsibilities over their membership. It is a constitutional process that the government is obligated to respect.\nThe votes belong to the people. The Constitution defines the powers of government.
