The Federal Police signed their 16-page request to seize Flávio’s passports at 9:16 pm Sunday, just seven minutes after the electoral court confirmed his defeat. Moraes approved it later that night without consulting the federal Prosecutor General. Moraes’s own colleagues were surprised by the passport order and feared it would raise tensions, columnist Mônica Bergamo reported.\n“It’s not just Flavio Bolsonaro who fled Brazil to escape the tyrannical persecution of Alexandre de Moraes and the pro-Lula wing of the Supreme Court,” notes journalist Glenn Greenwald, who has broken major stories about Moraes’ abuses of power. “There are dozens of Brazilian exiles who have done the same: dissident journalists, activists, bloggers, and politicians who all fled to avoid certain prison for political opposition.”\n \nSupporters of Lula and Moraes point to evidence of potential wrongdoing relating to an association with Tomaz, the man who moved 5 billion reais ($895 million) through an online betting platform in a single year. Police found emails indicating Flávio shared a Santander account with Tomaz and was registered as “master user” of an account belonging to Alcazar Holdings, a Tomaz company in Madeira, Portugal. On May 22, 2025, as the pension fraud scandal broke, someone asked the bank to remove Flávio’s name, which investigators found suspicious. On May 25, 2023, Loterj president Hazenclever Cançado signed the authorization that made Tomaz’s Rede Loto the first betting company licensed in Brazil (just in Rio), and Cançado got his job on Flávio’s recommendation, Folha reported, citing people familiar with Loterj’s procedures. The men deny sharing a bank account or co-owning a betting business. No document names Flávio as a shareholder of Alcazar or the other relevant entities. The emails do not show who moved money in the account or whether the senator received illicit funds. Flávio admits he solicited the Dark Horse money but says it was private sponsorship with no public funds, which police have not disproved.
